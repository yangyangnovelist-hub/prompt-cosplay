01 / The distinction

Stop grading the costume.

A role can sound senior, list every fashionable tool, and still be an untested hypothesis. PromptCosplay keeps three kinds of evidence separate so prose cannot borrow credibility from proof it never earned.

01 / 03

Contract

Does the role define concrete steps, outputs, tools, boundaries, failure handling, and acceptance gates? Good instructions matter. They are still only the specification.

02 / 03

Proof

Is there a role-specific eval, benchmark, or test? A generic repository CI badge does not prove that every persona can perform its advertised job.

03 / 03

Use

Did the role fire in a real session, and did it finish without error? PromptCosplay can merge observed-use evidence instead of inventing another transcript parser.

03 / The verdicts

Four exits.
No vibes.

Hover a verdict to retune the evidence field. Each label makes a different claim—and every claim is deliberately narrow.

01 / 04 Ship

Strong execution contract plus role-specific proof or successful observed use, with no blocking skill errors.

↗ 02 / 04 Prove

Potentially useful and operationally specified, but the advertised capability is still a claim.

↗ 03 / 04 Merge

Substantial lexical overlap with a stronger role. Consolidate the prompt surface; preserve the useful parts.

↗ 04 / 04 Bury

Too little operational substance to justify the prompt surface. PromptCosplay reports it; it never deletes it.

↗

The audit
audits itself.

No fake omniscience. Static contract scores measure whether instructions are operational—not whether a model follows them. Missing proof means “not detected,” not “can never work.”

PromptCosplay reuses SkillDoctor for conformance and security, SkillReaper for observed use, and promptfoo for behavioral A/B execution.
